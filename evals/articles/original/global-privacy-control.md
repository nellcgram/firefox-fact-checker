# Global Privacy Control

Introduced in [Firefox version](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/find-what-version-firefox-you-are-using) 120, you can make use of [Global Privacy Control](https://globalprivacycontrol.org/) (GPC) to automatically notify websites not to sell or share information about your browsing session on that website.

GPC operates as a “Do Not Sell” mechanism in some US states such as California, Colorado and Connecticut.

It may also be used to indicate an opt-out of targeted advertising or general request to limit the sale or sharing of your personal data in those jurisdictions, as well as in jurisdictions such as the EU, UK, Nevada, Utah and Virginia.

> **Disclaimer:** GPC is a signal, not a blocker. Some websites may ignore it. If you want stronger protection, combine it with [Enhanced Tracking Protection](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/enhanced-tracking-protection-firefox-desktop).

Mozilla added this feature to give you more choice and control. If you’d rather not send the signal, you can turn it off anytime.

## Enable Global Privacy Control

1. In the Menu bar at the top of the screen, click Firefox and select Settings (or Preferences, in some cases).

2. Select Privacy and security on the left.

3. Go down to the Additional protections section.

4. Click the box next to Tell websites not to sell or share my data to add a check mark.

Firefox saves your changes automatically.

## Disable Global Privacy Control

If you don’t want to send the GPC signal:

1. In the Menu bar at the top of the screen, click Firefox and select Settings (or Preferences, in some cases).

2. Select Privacy and security on the left.

3. Go down to the Additional protections section.

4. Click the box next to Tell websites not to sell or share my data to remove the check mark.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does this stop all tracking?** No. GPC only sends a request signal. Some websites may still track you.

**Can websites ignore GPC?** Yes. Outside certain regions, compliance is optional.

**Does GPC replace Do Not Track?** The option to enable the “Do Not Track” feature in Firefox settings was removed in [Firefox version](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/find-what-version-firefox-you-are-using) 135 (see [this article](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/how-do-i-turn-do-not-track-feature) for details). GPC is newer and has stronger recognition in laws such as the CCPA and GDPR.

**Will enabling this break websites?** Usually no. It just sends a privacy signal. If you notice issues, you can turn it off.
